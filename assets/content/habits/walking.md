# Go for a Walk

## What it is

Walking — especially outdoors — is one of the most underrated mental health tools available. It's free, requires no equipment, and the science backing it is overwhelming.

## Why it works

Walking increases serotonin and dopamine, reduces cortisol, and promotes neurogenesis (new brain cell growth). A 20-minute walk in nature lowers activity in the brain region linked to rumination — the mental loop of anxious or depressive thoughts.

## How to start

1. Pick a consistent time: morning walks are energizing, evening walks help unwind
2. Leave your phone in your pocket — or better yet, at home
3. Walk at a pace where you can still hold a conversation
4. Notice your surroundings: sounds, textures, light

## Tips

- You don't need a park — any outdoor space counts
- Walking meetings are productive and refreshing
- Pair it with a podcast or audiobook if silence feels uncomfortable at first
- Even 10 minutes is effective

## 2-Minute Version

Step outside your front door. Walk to the end of the street and back. That's enough.

*Use this version when you're just getting started. Once the habit feels natural, gradually increase the time.*
