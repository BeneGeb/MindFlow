# Sleep Hygiene

## What it is
Sleep hygiene is a set of habits and practices that support consistently good sleep. It's not one magic trick — it's an environment and routine you build around your sleep.

## Why it works
During sleep, your brain clears metabolic waste products including those linked to Alzheimer's disease. It consolidates memories, repairs tissue, and resets your emotional regulation. Poor sleep doesn't just make you tired — it impairs judgment, increases anxiety, reduces impulse control, and weakens your immune system.

## Core principles

### Wind-Down Routine
1. Dim lights 1–2 hours before bed — light suppresses melatonin
2. Avoid screens or use blue-light blocking mode after 9pm
3. Lower room temperature (18–20°C / 65–68°F is optimal for sleep)
4. Do the same sequence every night: your brain learns to associate it with sleep

### Morning Anchor
- Wake at the same time every day — even weekends
- Get sunlight in your eyes within 30 minutes of waking
- These two habits are the most powerful regulators of your circadian rhythm

### What to avoid
- Caffeine after 2pm (it has a 6-hour half-life)
- Alcohol — it disrupts REM sleep even if it helps you fall asleep
- Intense exercise within 2 hours of sleep

## 2-Minute Version
Put your phone on the charger in another room, outside your bedroom. This one change removes the biggest sleep disruptor for most people.

## Atomic Habits tip
Design your environment for sleep. Blackout curtains, a cool room, and no screens are not luxuries — they're the scaffolding that makes the habit easy.
