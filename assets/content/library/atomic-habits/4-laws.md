# The 4 Laws of Behavior Change

From *Atomic Habits* by James Clear

Every habit — good or bad — follows the same neurological loop: **Cue → Craving → Response → Reward**. To build a good habit, make each step easier. To break a bad one, make each step harder.

---

## Law 1: Make It Obvious
Your brain is wired to respond to cues in your environment. If you don't see it, you won't do it.

**How to apply it:**
- Put your journal on your pillow
- Set your workout clothes out the night before
- Use a habit tracker — seeing the empty checkbox is itself a cue
- Create an **Implementation Intention**: *"I will [habit] at [time] in [location]."*

---

## Law 2: Make It Attractive
You do things because of the feeling you expect to get, not just the outcome. The more attractive a habit feels, the more likely you are to do it.

**How to apply it:**
- Pair an activity you want to do with one you need to do (*"I only listen to my favorite podcast while walking"*)
- Join a community where your desired behavior is the norm
- Focus on the identity, not the outcome: *"I'm becoming someone who takes care of their mind"*

---

## Law 3: Make It Easy
The amount of effort a habit requires determines how often you'll do it. Reduce friction ruthlessly.

**How to apply it:**
- Use the **2-Minute Rule**: scale every habit down to a 2-minute version to start
- Reduce the number of steps between you and the habit
- Design your environment so the good choice is the default choice
- Prime your environment: fill a glass of water before bed so it's there in the morning

---

## Law 4: Make It Satisfying
The brain prioritizes immediate rewards over delayed ones. Habits that feel good *now* get repeated.

**How to apply it:**
- Track your habits — the act of checking off feels rewarding
- Never miss twice — one missed day is a mistake, two is a new habit
- Use small celebrations: a fist pump, a moment of appreciation — it works
- Focus on the identity reinforcement: *"I'm someone who meditates. I just proved it again."*

---

## The Core Insight
You don't rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems. MindFlow is your system.
