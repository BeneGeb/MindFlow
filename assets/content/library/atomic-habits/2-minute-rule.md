# The 2-Minute Rule

From *Atomic Habits* by James Clear

---

## The Idea
When you start a new habit, it should take less than 2 minutes to do.

"Read before bed each night" becomes "Read one page."
"Do yoga" becomes "Get out my yoga mat."
"Meditate" becomes "Sit in my meditation spot."

This sounds too simple to matter. It's not.

---

## Why It Works
The hardest part of any habit is starting. Once you've begun, continuing feels natural. The 2-Minute Rule removes the activation energy required to start.

It also exploits a psychological truth: **showing up is the skill**. Before you can optimize a habit, you need to establish it. Before you can be consistent, you need to exist in the practice.

You're not trying to do the habit perfectly. You're casting a vote for the identity of someone who does this habit at all.

---

## The Gateway Habit
Think of the 2-minute version not as the full habit, but as the **gateway habit** — the ritual that begins a longer practice.

- Open your journal → often leads to writing for 10 minutes
- Get out your mat → often leads to a full yoga session
- Put on your running shoes → often leads to a run

But it doesn't have to. On hard days, the 2-minute version alone is a win.

## MindFlow's 2-Minute Versions

| Habit | 2-Minute Version |
|-------|-----------------|
| Meditate | 3 deep, conscious breaths |
| Journal | Write one sentence about how you feel |
| Walk | Step outside for fresh air |
| Exercise | 10 squats and 10 push-ups |
| Yoga | Child's Pose for 2 minutes |
| Breathing | One round of Box Breathing |
| Sleep Hygiene | Put your phone in another room |
| Digital Detox | Close all apps and put phone face down |

---

## The Long Game
Standardize before you optimize. Make the habit automatic before you make it ambitious. Two minutes now leads to twenty minutes later — but only because you started.
