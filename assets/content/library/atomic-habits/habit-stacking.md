# Habit Stacking

From *Atomic Habits* by James Clear

---

## The Idea
Habit stacking links a new habit to an existing one, using the established behavior as the cue for the new one.

**Formula:** *"After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."*

Examples:
- *"After I pour my morning coffee, I will meditate for 5 minutes."*
- *"After I sit down at my desk, I will write one journal sentence."*
- *"After I brush my teeth at night, I will do my breathing exercise."*

---

## Why It Works
Your existing habits are already deeply wired neural pathways. Rather than building a new habit from scratch in isolation, you're piggybacking on momentum that's already there.

The old habit becomes the cue. The new habit becomes the reward that follows.

---

## How to Build a Stack

1. **List your anchor habits** — things you do automatically every day without thinking (make coffee, brush teeth, sit down for lunch, get into bed)
2. **Choose a new habit** — something you want to add
3. **Write the stack formula** — *"After I [anchor], I will [new habit]."*
4. **Place a physical cue** if needed — put your journal next to the coffee maker

---

## In MindFlow
You implement Habit Stacking yourself through planning. When you schedule habits in the Planner, stack them back-to-back:

- 07:00 → Morning coffee (your anchor)
- 07:05 → Meditate (5 min)
- 07:10 → Journal (5 min)

The Planner makes the order visual and concrete — the closest thing to a digital habit stack.

---

## Common Mistakes
- **Stack too many habits at once** — start with one new habit per stack
- **Choose a weak anchor** — use habits you do every day without fail
- **Make the new habit too hard** — combine Habit Stacking with the 2-Minute Rule for best results

---

## The Result
Over time, your stacked habits start to feel like a single, flowing routine. You stop thinking about each one individually — the stack runs on autopilot.
