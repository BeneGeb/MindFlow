# Who You're Becoming

Most people approach habits as a list of things they want to do. But lasting change tends to come from a deeper place: a shift in how you see yourself.

## Two Levels of Change

There's a difference between:
- *"I want to meditate more"* — a goal focused on an outcome
- *"I'm someone who meditates"* — an identity focused on who you are

Both might lead to the same action, but they behave very differently under pressure. Goals are easy to postpone. Identity is harder to betray.

When you act in line with who you believe you are, the motivation becomes internal — not something you need to generate from the outside.

## Identity Is Built by Evidence

You don't decide to have a new identity and then feel it. It works the other way around: small actions gradually accumulate into a belief.

Every time you complete a habit, you cast a small vote for a certain kind of person. One meditation doesn't make you a meditator. But 30 consecutive days of showing up — even briefly — starts to feel like evidence that this is just who you are now.

This is why consistency matters more than perfection. Missed days don't erase who you're becoming. Getting back on track reinforces it.

## Practical Questions to Ask

When you're deciding whether to follow through on a habit, try shifting the question:

- Instead of *"Do I feel like doing this?"*
- Ask *"Is this what someone like me does?"*

It sounds subtle, but it reframes the decision from willpower to identity.

## Letting Go of the "All or Nothing" Trap

One of the biggest barriers to identity-based habits is the belief that a missed day means failure. It doesn't. The goal isn't a perfect record — it's a general direction.

Missing once is an accident. Missing twice starts to become a pattern. But returning after a miss? That's what actually defines you.

> The goal is not to be perfect. The goal is to be consistent enough that the habit becomes part of how you describe yourself.
